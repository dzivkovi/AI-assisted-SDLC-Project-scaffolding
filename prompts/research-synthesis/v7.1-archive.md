# Multi-Research Decision Synthesis — Verification-Hardened, Decision-First v7.1

> **V7.1 - ARCHIVED, do not run.** Verbatim from `Multi-AI_Research_synthesis_prompt_v7.1.md` (2026-07-13), only line endings normalized to LF.
>
> **Why it is here:** it is the named exemplar of the anti-pattern - a new rule added after every observed failure until the prompt became a policy manual. It grew to 3,600 words immediately after prompt bloat had been identified as the problem, and scored 3.5/10 on followability, the lowest of any version. Kept as a checklist of failure modes worth designing against, not as something to send to a model.

You are synthesizing several independent AI research reports into a concise, defensible decision brief.

Your goal is **not** to combine all available information. Your goal is to identify the smallest set of well-supported findings required to make a good decision while preserving valuable minority insights.

A polished answer is not sufficient. Every decision-changing claim must be traceable to evidence that supports the precise claim being made.

The internal analysis may be detailed. The default visible output must remain concise and decision-focused.

# Analysis Method

## 1. Resolve the Decision Context

Convert the research task into three to seven decision questions.

Examples:

* What must a viable solution provide?
* Which options satisfy the non-negotiable constraints?
* Which option best fits each major usage role?
* What claims could materially change the recommendation?
* What is the least expensive reliable way to validate the choice?

Do not introduce dimensions unrelated to the original task.

### When constraints or preferences are blank

If `HARD CONSTRAINTS` or `PREFERENCES` are blank:

1. Extract only constraints explicitly stated or logically unavoidable from the research task and decision context.
2. Label each one **Inferred from task**, rather than user-confirmed.
3. Treat other useful dimensions as evaluation criteria, not hard gates.
4. Do not fail a candidate on an inferred gate unless violating it would clearly make the candidate unusable for the stated purpose.
5. State any assumption that materially affects the ranking.

Do not silently invent licence, budget, hosting, enterprise-administration or security requirements merely because they are commonly important.

---

## 2. Evaluate Report Adherence

Evaluate Reports A, B and C anonymously before considering model identity.

For each report identify:

* Directness
* Coverage of the actual task
* Coverage of stated constraints
* Evidence quality
* Actionability
* Important omissions
* Unsupported certainty
* Unnecessary material

Use **High / Medium / Low** rather than arbitrary numerical precision.

Do not reward:

* Length
* Polished language
* Citation volume
* Popularity metrics
* Confident tone
* Familiar vendor names

A concise report that finds one decision-changing fact may be more valuable than a comprehensive market inventory.

---

## 3. Build an Internal Canonical Claim Ledger

Normalize overlapping statements into distinct claims.

For every decision-relevant claim record internally:

| Claim | Claim Class | Reports Finding It | Underlying Sources | Independent Source Count | Evidence Status | Decision Impact |
| ----- | ----------- | ------------------ | ------------------ | -----------------------: | --------------- | --------------- |

### Claim classes

* **Verified fact:** directly observable and supported by the cited source
* **Comparative claim:** one option is better, faster, safer, more mature or otherwise superior
* **Interpretation:** a reasoned reading of verified facts
* **Inference:** a conclusion extending beyond what the source states directly
* **Recommendation:** an action derived from facts, context and trade-offs
* **Forecast:** a claim about likely future behaviour
* **Assumption:** an unverified condition used to continue the analysis

### Evidence status

* **Verified — primary:** directly confirmed in the authoritative source
* **Corroborated — independent:** supported by at least two genuinely independent credible sources
* **Independent published evidence:** published by a party independent of the evaluated product
* **Published product-team evidence:** documented or benchmarked by the product’s authors or vendor
* **Vendor marketing claim:** asserted by a vendor without adequate reproducible support
* **Single-source:** supported by one non-authoritative or non-independent source
* **Disputed:** credible sources materially conflict
* **Unverified:** reported but not adequately supported
* **Contradicted:** reliable evidence shows the claim is incorrect
* **Stale:** previously true, but current status is uncertain or has changed

### Claim-ledger rules

* Multiple reports citing the same underlying source count as one source.
* Multiple vendor-controlled pages count as one source family.
* Model agreement measures prominence, not truth.
* A 1/3 verified claim may outrank a 3/3 unverified claim.
* An omission is not disagreement.
* Product existence or feature support does not establish quality, superiority, security approval, operational fitness or maintenance continuity.
* A product-team paper is primary evidence for what its authors tested, but not independent validation of superiority.
* Separate verified facts from interpretations and inferences throughout the analysis.

The full claim ledger is internal in Decision mode. Include it only in Audit mode.

---

## 4. Analyse Convergence and Outliers

For each important finding track two separate dimensions.

### Discovery support

* 3/3 reports
* 2/3 reports
* 1/3 report
* 0/3 reports; discovered during synthesis verification

### Evidence strength

* Verified — primary
* Corroborated — independent
* Independent published evidence
* Published product-team evidence
* Weak
* Disputed
* Unknown

Preserve a minority or newly discovered insight only when at least one condition applies:

* It could materially change the decision.
* It exposes a hidden risk.
* It introduces a meaningfully different solution category.
* It suggests a low-cost, high-value experiment.
* It is supported by unusually strong evidence.
* It reveals that a leading recommendation violates an important constraint.

Do not preserve an observation merely because it is different.

Consensus should be described as **discovery convergence**, not proof.

---

## 5. Verify Decision-Changing Claims

When current verification is available, verify only the claims most likely to affect the decision:

1. Claims supporting the leading recommendation
2. Claims that eliminate or materially demote a candidate
3. Valuable 1/3 outliers
4. Direct conflicts among reports
5. Current licence, pricing, security, compatibility, configuration, availability and maintenance claims
6. Comparative claims that materially affect ranking
7. Previously verified claims that may have changed

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Official documentation, repository, licence, regulation, dataset, standard or original paper
2. Independent technical evaluation with a disclosed method
3. Reputable reporting
4. User or community evidence with reproducible details
5. Vendor marketing
6. Aggregators and SEO comparison pages

Vendor-controlled sources may establish their own:

* Features
* Configuration
* Licence
* Release state
* Documented limitations
* Benchmark methodology

They must not independently establish:

* General superiority
* Production quality
* Security approval
* Customer outcomes
* Reliability across environments
* Fitness for the user’s specific context

---

### 5.1 Verification integrity

For every decision-changing verification:

1. Write down the exact claim being checked.
2. Open the underlying source.
3. Locate the exact passage, table, configuration line, licence clause, release entry or result supporting it.
4. Record what the source establishes.
5. Record what the source does **not** establish.
6. Record material caveats, scope conditions, methodology limitations and conflicting evidence.
7. Recheck the final wording after drafting to ensure it did not become broader or stronger than the evidence.

If the full source cannot be inspected, label the claim **Unverified** or **Single-source**.

Do not upgrade a claim because multiple reports repeated it.

---

### 5.2 Comparative claims

Never label a comparison as verified unless the evidence explicitly evaluates every named option under relevant and reasonably comparable conditions.

Examples:

* A benchmark against Product X does not prove superiority over Products Y and Z.
* A feature matrix written by Vendor A does not prove Vendor A is better.
* A successful test on small repositories does not establish large-repository performance.
* One reported failure proves that the failure occurred, not its prevalence.
* Popularity, stars, downloads, funding and customer logos do not establish technical quality.
* Feature quantity does not establish answer quality.
* A lower operating cost does not prove a better overall choice.

When a ranking is based on interpretation rather than direct comparison, label it **Interpretation** or **Inference**.

---

### 5.3 Version and time pinning

For claims that may change over time, record internally:

* Source owner
* Repository, document or product
* Exact page, file or release inspected
* Branch, tag, release, version or commit SHA when available
* Verification date

Time-sensitive claims include:

* Configuration defaults
* Licences
* Releases
* Pricing
* APIs
* Product status
* Maintenance activity
* Security controls
* Supported integrations
* Model availability

Prefer immutable links or version-pinned sources.

A mutable `main` branch may be used for a current-state claim only when the verification date is stated.

Do not present an archived page, fork, package copy, translated mirror, third-party deployment example or older configuration as the current official state.

---

### 5.4 Fact, interpretation and inference discipline

Use these categories:

```text
VERIFIED FACT:
What the source directly states or demonstrates.

INTERPRETATION:
What the verified facts plausibly mean.

INFERENCE:
A conclusion extending beyond the direct evidence.

UNKNOWN:
What remains unresolved.

NOT ESTABLISHED:
A stronger conclusion that the evidence does not support.
```

Example:

```text
VERIFIED FACT:
The same organization maintains two active repositories.

INTERPRETATION:
The product direction is evolving.

INFERENCE:
Maintainer effort may be divided.

NOT ESTABLISHED:
The older repository is abandoned or will be discontinued.
```

Example:

```text
VERIFIED FACT:
A plugin reuses an existing coding-agent runtime.

INTERPRETATION:
Deployment overhead may be lower than for a standalone service.

NOT ESTABLISHED:
The plugin requires no additional security, privacy, supply-chain or egress review.
```

Do not convert a reasonable inference into a verified fact merely because it sounds likely.

Use neutral language. Prefer:

> “Defaults to an external embedding endpoint and may transmit source-derived content outside the tenant.”

Avoid unnecessarily loaded wording such as:

> “Leaks the code.”

unless an actual vulnerability or unauthorized disclosure has been established.

---

### 5.5 Security and privacy claims

Do not reduce security to self-hosting or model location.

For security-sensitive recommendations, distinguish at minimum:

* Repository credential handling
* Code and prompt egress
* Generation-model endpoint
* Embedding-model endpoint
* Reranking or auxiliary-model endpoints
* Telemetry, analytics and crash reporting
* Update checks and external runtime assets
* Storage of embeddings, indexes, graphs, generated documents and chat history
* Authentication and authorization
* Tenant isolation
* Secret scanning and prompt filtering
* Software supply-chain and plugin review
* Network controls and egress restrictions

A locally hosted application calling an external model is not fully local.

Reuse of an approved model endpoint does not automatically approve a new plugin or application.

A documented default that violates the user’s constraint is a deployment precondition or risk—not necessarily a product vulnerability.

---

### 5.6 Benchmark and research evidence

For every benchmark or paper used to rank candidates, record:

* Who conducted it
* Their relationship to the evaluated product
* Exact systems compared
* Dataset or repository sample
* Scale and representativeness
* Metrics
* Judging method
* Model versions and configurations
* Whether results were independently reproduced
* Important limitations
* Whether LLM-as-judge was used
* Whether the authors created the benchmark

Use these evidence labels:

* **Independent replicated evidence**
* **Independent published evidence**
* **Published product-team evidence**
* **Vendor marketing claim**

Do not describe product-team research as third-party or independent evidence.

A product-team benchmark may still be useful, but its scope and provenance must be explicit.

---

### 5.7 Licence-state discipline

Distinguish:

* Explicit licence file present
* Licence stated only in README
* Licence declared in package metadata
* Multiple inconsistent licence indicators
* Source-available licence
* Open-source licence
* Dual or commercial licence
* No identifiable licence grant

Do not automatically mark a candidate **Fail** merely because a conventional `LICENSE` file is missing when the README or package metadata declares a licence.

Instead use:

* **Pass:** clear and consistent licence grant
* **Conditional:** declared licence exists but repository hygiene or consistency is incomplete
* **Fail:** licence terms clearly conflict with a hard constraint
* **Unknown:** no reliable licence grant can be established

When legal interpretation matters, recommend confirmation by the client’s legal or procurement team rather than making a legal conclusion.

---

### 5.8 Contradiction and staleness check

Before finalizing a decision-changing claim:

1. Search for current contradictory evidence.
2. Confirm that the source is current and canonical.
3. Prefer the newest authoritative source for current-state facts.
4. If authoritative sources disagree, report the conflict.
5. If a claim was historically true but its present status is unclear, label it **Stale**.
6. Recheck all claims that changed a ranking, gate result or recommendation.

State what was verified, what changed and what remains unknown.

---

## 6. Apply Hard Gates

Evaluate candidates against confirmed or unavoidable constraints before scoring preferences.

| Candidate | Gate 1 | Gate 2 | Gate 3 | Gate 4 | Result | Evidence |
| --------- | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ | -------- |

Results:

* **Pass:** current evidence supports compliance
* **Conditional:** requires a specific unresolved confirmation or configuration
* **Fail:** current evidence shows the constraint is not met
* **Unknown:** insufficient evidence

Rules:

* Do not convert Unknown into Pass.
* Do not average away a failed hard constraint.
* Distinguish user-confirmed gates from inferred gates.
* A configurable default should normally produce Conditional, not Fail, when the constraint can be reliably satisfied through documented configuration.
* An incomplete licence declaration should normally produce Conditional, not Fail, unless the available terms clearly conflict with the requirement.
* Only candidates that pass or have clearly resolvable conditional status proceed to comparative recommendation.

The full gate matrix is internal in Decision mode unless it is required to explain the recommendation.

---

## 7. Rank by Usage Role

Do not force one universal winner when candidates solve different jobs.

Where applicable, identify:

* Best overall
* Best for the user’s primary workflow
* Best low-complexity option
* Best enterprise or shared-platform option
* Best technically advanced option
* Best complementary tool
* Best for large-scale use
* Worth watching
* Not recommended for this use case

For every recommended candidate state:

* Best use
* Why it wins
* Main compromise
* Who should not choose it
* Evidence confidence
* Adoption status
* What would change the recommendation

Use these adoption statuses:

* **Recommended for immediate trial**
* **Recommended for production adoption**
* **Recommended for production after conditions are met**
* **Recommended for further validation**
* **Worth watching**
* **Not recommended for this use case**

A candidate must not be called the winner merely because it is the cheapest experiment.

Distinguish:

```text
Fastest to test
Best demonstrated fit
Best production candidate
```

If preference weights were not supplied, use directional judgments instead of invented weighted totals.

# Output Contract

## Decision Mode — Default

Produce only Outputs A, B and C.

Do not include the complete claim ledger, report scorecard, full outlier inventory or extended appendix unless the user requests Audit mode.

---

## Output A — Decision Brief

Target: **600–800 words**.
Hard maximum: **900 words**.

Include:

### 1. Decision

One clear paragraph answering the original question.

Distinguish:

* Immediate trial
* Production recommendation
* Conditions that must be resolved

### 2. Top Findings

Maximum five.

For each include:

* Finding
* Discovery support
* Evidence status
* Fact, interpretation or inference label when material

### 3. Recommended Choices by Role

Maximum five candidates.

Include:

* Candidate
* Best use
* Main compromise
* Confidence
* Adoption status

### 4. Do Now

Maximum three concrete actions.

### 5. Do Not Do

Maximum three avoidable mistakes.

### 6. Critical Unknown

State the single unresolved issue most likely to change the decision.

### 7. What Would Change My Mind

State the evidence or test result that would materially alter the recommendation.

---

## Output B — Compact Comparison

Maximum:

* Seven candidates
* Eight columns
* One concise sentence per cell
* Approximately 300–400 words total

Suggested structure:

| Candidate | Best For | Gate Status | Key Strength | Main Blocker | Evidence | Adoption Status | Recommendation |
| --------- | -------- | ----------- | ------------ | ------------ | -------- | --------------- | -------------- |

Do not repeat explanations already given in the Decision Brief. Refer to them only with compact labels.

---

## Output C — Decision-Changing Verification Notes

Include a maximum of **three** verification notes.

Select only claims that:

* Changed the winner
* Changed a hard-gate result
* Exposed a serious hidden risk
* Corrected a material error repeated by the reports
* Preserved a valuable outlier that would otherwise be lost

Use:

| Exact Claim Checked | Result | Source State | Evidence Class | Source Establishes | Source Does Not Establish | Effect on Decision |
| ------------------- | ------ | ------------ | -------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------- | ------------------ |

For mutable technical sources, include the version, branch, tag, release, commit or verification date.

If fewer than three decision-changing verifications exist, include fewer.

---

## Audit Mode — Optional

When Audit mode is explicitly requested, append only the relevant sections below.

### Output D — Outlier Nuggets

Maximum five.

```text
Insight:
Why it matters:
Found by:
Claim class:
Evidence status:
Recommended treatment:
```

### Output E — Claim Ledger

Include only decision-relevant claims.

### Output F — Report Assessment

Briefly compare the reports on:

* Directness
* Evidence quality
* Actionability
* Unsupported certainty
* Most valuable contribution
* Most consequential failure

Do not assign fixed model personalities from one research run.

### Output G — Evidence Appendix

May include:

* Extended verification notes
* Exact source excerpts
* Benchmark details
* Excluded candidates
* Full hard-gate matrix
* Contradictory evidence
* Unresolved research gaps

Do not repeat the Decision Brief verbatim in the appendix.

---

# One-Home Rule

Every major finding must have one primary home.

Use this order:

1. Decision Brief for findings required to understand the recommendation
2. Verification Notes for evidence that materially changed the decision
3. Audit appendix for supporting detail

Do not restate the same finding in:

* Top Findings
* Comparison table
* Outlier Nuggets
* Verification Notes
* Report scorecard

The comparison table may summarize a finding in a short phrase, but must not explain it again.

Before finalizing, remove repetitive explanations.

---

# Compression Rules

* Lead with the answer.
* Preserve verification rigor internally.
* Expose only evidence required to understand or defend the decision.
* Do not include background history unless it changes the recommendation.
* Do not write generic market commentary.
* Do not produce a roadmap when three immediate actions are sufficient.
* Do not assign model personalities based on one run.
* Do not treat citation quantity as evidence quality.
* Do not use popularity metrics as proof of technical quality.
* Do not present assumptions, interpretations or inferences as verified facts.
* Do not describe product-team benchmarks as independent evidence.
* Do not infer enterprise approval from self-hostability or local execution.
* Do not treat a configurable external default as an unfixable product failure.
* Do not treat incomplete licence hygiene as definitive absence of a licence without inspecting all canonical declarations.
* Do not use “verified,” “confirmed,” “proven,” “best” or “secure” more broadly than the evidence supports.
* Use exact dates for time-sensitive claims.
* Pin mutable technical sources to a version, commit or verification date.
* Label unresolved uncertainty explicitly.
* Prefer neutral, technically precise wording.
* Prefer one sharp sentence over an explanatory paragraph.
* Stop when the decision is sufficiently supported.
